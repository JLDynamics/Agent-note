# Durable capture

Create a note that will still be useful when found without the current
conversation. Synthesize the user-provided information around its lasting
meaning and context; do not merely shorten the text or produce a generic
summary. Be concise without discarding details that determine how the note
should be understood or used.

## Preserve the useful shape

- **Idea:** preserve the idea, why it matters or the problem it solves, and any
  meaningful limits or constraints. Include a next step only when one is
  present in the source; do not invent one.
- **Decision:** preserve the choice and the reason or context for it.
- **Preference:** preserve the preference and when it applies.
- **Other durable information:** preserve the context needed to understand why
  it may matter later, without padding the note with incidental wording.

Use a specific title that identifies the subject. Do not label every note
“Summary”; name the idea, decision, preference, project, or topic instead.

## Organize for retrieval

Keep the existing frontmatter fields exactly: `title`, `date`, and `tags`. Do
not invent additional fields.

Choose a clear, subject-specific title. Use only a few useful tags, up to the
existing eight-tag limit; the limit is not a target. Favor relevant topic tags
and optionally one kind tag such as `idea`, `decision`, `preference`, or
`session-handoff` when it materially improves retrieval.

Run `tags` and reuse established tags when they fit. Do not force a project tag,
kind tag, or arbitrary taxonomy. Never add tags merely for completeness, and
avoid duplicate, near-synonym, or noisy tags. Treat tags as a retrieval aid,
not a complex classification system.

## Preserve uncertainty

Distinguish established facts from assumptions, proposals, possibilities, and
unresolved questions. Retain qualifications and attribution when they matter.
Do not convert speculation into fact, resolve ambiguity without evidence, or
claim that a proposed next step is committed.

Write only from the supplied source and established context. Never invent
reasons, constraints, outcomes, or next steps to make a note look complete.
